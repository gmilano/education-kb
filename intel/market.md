## Demand signal — adoption has already outrun governance everywhere

| Signal (search-channel) | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Higher-ed students globally using AI in their studies | **`86%`** | Digital Education Council |
| Universities automating admin work (enrolment, scheduling, plagiarism) | **`90%`** | reported in sector statistics round-ups |
| 🔴 Faculty saying their institution has a **formal AI policy** | **~`25%`** | faculty survey, reported |
| LATAM universities using AI | **`87%`** · only **`26%`** with a strategy · **`9%`** evaluating it | UNESCO IESALC |
| LATAM institutions with guidelines **and** capacity to apply them | 🔴 **<`10%`** | UNESCO |

🟢 **This gap is the product.** The sector's binding constraint in 2026 is not model capability and
not teacher willingness — it is **governance, evidence and procurement readiness**. An offer built on
"we will make your existing AI use defensible" addresses a measured deficit; one built on "we will
bring you AI" addresses a need that is already met.

## Opportunities by region

### North America

🟢 **The regulated-grading market, and it is live now rather than pending.**

- **Instruments, not tallies.** 🔴 Trackers disagree on volume (`134` bills / `31` states versus
  `68` / `27` with `10` enacted), so cite the instrument: **North Carolina** — model policy due
  `2026-12-31`, local policies `2027-06-30`, teacher training `2028-06-30`; **New York A 9190** —
  would restrict classroom AI to grade 9+, with diagnostic and special-education exceptions;
  **Maryland** — state guidance, local AI policies and named AI coordinators; **Oklahoma's
  Responsible Technology in Schools Act** — bars AI used *solely* for grading, discipline or
  promotion; **Ohio** — school AI-policy mandate already in force; **Utah** — regulatory sandbox.
- **Federal pull:** the U.S. Department of Education finalised a rule prioritising federal education
  grants for AI-related projects, dated `2026-04-13`. 🟢 **Money with an AI condition attached is the
  strongest procurement signal in any region this pass.**
- `35+` states have official education-department AI guidance (as of June 2026).
- **California:** an OpenAI / Common Sense Media ballot initiative, the *Parents & Kids Safe AI Act*,
  could set a de-facto national floor for how AI firms treat minors.
- **Non-binding but load-bearing:** the student-authored *STUDENTS FIRST Act of 2026* (AASA, all 50
  states) rejects both a ban and unrestricted adoption — useful framing in a school-board room.
- **OSS anchors here:** Canvas (AGPL-3.0), Sakai (🟢 ECL-2.0), `Khan/perseus` (🟢 MIT),
  `CAHLR/OATutor` (🟢 MIT, UC Berkeley), `jupyter/nbgrader` (🟢 BSD-3),
  `janestreet/agent_skills` (🟢 MIT).
- 🟢 **The offer:** human-in-the-loop grading with an audit trail, built on nbgrader + OATutor
  mastery estimation, scoped so that AI never *solely* determines a grade — which is precisely the
  line Oklahoma drew and others are drawing. See `compose/patterns.md` → `P787-1`.

### EMEA

🟢 **The compliance-deadline market, with a 13-month runway and a measured supply gap.**

- **The dates that matter.** Art. 5 prohibitions and Art. 4 AI-literacy duties have applied since
  `2025-02-02` — **the emotion-recognition ban in education is already in force.** Art. 50
  transparency applies from `2026-08-02`. 🟢 **High-risk obligations moved:**
  **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** (*Digital Omnibus on AI*), OJ `2026-07-24`, in force `2026-07-27`,
  shifted Annex III stand-alone high-risk — **education included** — from `2026-08-02` to
  **`2027-12-02`**; Annex I to `2028-08-02`.
- **What is high-risk in education (Annex III, point 3):** admissions decisions, evaluating learning
  outcomes, assigning education levels, and **exam proctoring**. AI determining a final grade is
  caught. Schools are **deployers**, and a public educational institution deploying high-risk AI owes
  an **Art. 27 FRIA** (fundamental rights impact assessment).
- 🟢 **Türkiye placed:** MEB's **YAZEK** ethical-declaration system (February 2026 — a declaration is
  filed *before* deployment); *AI in Education Policy Document and Action Plan 2025–2029* (updated
  `2026-04-13`); supreme and provincial ethics committees. 🔴 No AI statute.
- 🟢 **Israel placed:** Education Ministry **Project 720** — a 28-school pilot (2025–26) scaling past
  `180` schools toward national in three years; grade-based age-gating, mandatory teacher oversight,
  🔴 **the ministry approves every tool**; a 20-hour AI course in grades 8–9. 🔴 Guidelines, not statute.
- 🟢 **Africa:** Kenya (National AI Strategy 2025–2030, AI literacy in education, DigiKen) and
  Nigeria (NERDC directive on robotics/AI in the primary curriculum; National EdTech Strategy
  2025–2030) are placed; the AU's **CESA 2026–2035** treats AI as cross-cutting.
  🟢 **South Africa now settled** (`p786-C`): the draft National AI Policy was gazetted
  **Notice 3880 of 2026 on `2026-04-10`**, then **withdrawn on `2026-04-26`** by Minister Solly
  Malatsi of Communications and Digital Technologies after fabricated, AI-hallucinated citations were
  found in its reference list — reportedly **at least 6 of its 67 academic citations**; one source
  records abrogation `2026-04-27`, a day after withdrawal, so cite both dates. A seven-person panel
  is reviewing it, with **no published timeline**. The Department of Basic Education's 2026 digital
  education update refers to a **National AI in Education Framework**, 🔴 **unpublished, no timeline**.
  🔵 A national AI policy withdrawn because it cited AI-invented sources is the sharpest available
  argument for provenance controls in an education engagement.
- **Sovereign substrate, maturing fast:** OpenEuroLLM (open data, code, evaluation and weights),
  Germany's **SOOFI** (~€20M public funding, public-sector deployment targeted mid-2026), the
  Netherlands' **GPT-NL** (explicitly aimed at health, education and public services), Switzerland's
  **Apertus**, Fraunhofer's **Teuken**, Mistral (FR), Haystack (deepset, Berlin).
- **OSS anchors here:** 🟢 OpenOLAT (Apache-2.0, CH), Opencast (ECL-2.0), ILIAS (GPL-3.0, DE),
  Chamilo (GPL-3.0, BE/ES), H5P (GPL-3.0, NO), `hedyorg/hedy` (EUPL-1.2, `1,697★`, NL),
  OOAPI (EUPL-1.2, NL).
- 🔴 **The measured gap, stated rather than left silent:** searches for a European-origin
  **open-source education *agent*** returned sovereign-LLM infrastructure and compliance commentary
  **only** — no such repository surfaced. Europe is building the substrate and has not built the
  education layer on top of it.
  🟢 **That absence is the opportunity:** an EU-sovereign tutoring/assessment layer on GPT-NL,
  Apertus or OpenEuroLLM, FRIA-documented from the first commit, with `2027-12-02` as the deadline
  that sells it. See `compose/patterns.md` → `P787-4`.

### APAC

🟢 **The region with the strongest open-source agent *supply* and the weakest binding regulation.**

- **China:** no general AI law; binding sector measures instead (recommendation algorithms, deep
  synthesis, generative AI services, AI-content labelling). The amended **Cybersecurity Law, in force
  `2026-01-01`**, adds the country's first AI clause on development and safety supervision.
- **Japan:** promotion-first. The **AI Promotion Act** (2025) carries no penalties; the Cabinet
  adopted a second-phase **AI Basic Plan on `2026-07-14`**.
- **India:** no dedicated AI statute, but in July 2026 the government signalled it may pursue one.
  Amended **IT Rules require prominent labels and provenance markers on AI-generated content from
  `2026-02-20`** — 🟢 a provenance duty that directly touches generated courseware.
- 🟢 **Vietnam** is the region's one binding education hook: its 2026-07 rules **flag education as a
  high-risk AI sector, citing automated assessment.**
- **Public opinion (Ipsos Education Monitor 2026), support for banning AI in schools:** Indonesia
  `23%`, India `26%`, Japan `29%`, South Korea `31%` — 🟢 **a minority everywhere, so the social
  licence to deploy exists across the region.**
- **Commercial players:** TAL (CN) pivoted into AI learning hardware; ByteDance's **Gauth** became a
  top education app in the US; **PhysicsWallah** (IN) launched its own AI tutor.
- **OSS anchors here:** 🟢 `HKUDS/DeepTutor` (Apache-2.0, `58.5k★`, HK) and
  `THU-MAIC/OpenMAIC` (MIT, `43.4k★`, Tsinghua) — **the sector's two poles are both APAC-origin** —
  plus VeryMath, `china-k12-teacher-skills`, Math-Modeling-Agent-Flow, xuelian-agent, and
  `frappe/lms` (AGPL-3.0, IN).
- 🔴 **Named gap:** every APAC agent verified this pass is **China- or Hong-Kong-origin**. No
  Japan-, Korea- or India-origin permissively-granted education agent repository was found. India in
  particular has the platform layer (Frappe LMS) and commercial tutors (PhysicsWallah) but **no
  permissive agent layer** in this base.
- 🟢 **The offer:** localisation and compliance packaging of DeepTutor/OpenMAIC — both permissive,
  both already strong — against India's provenance-labelling duty and Vietnam's high-risk assessment
  listing. See `compose/patterns.md` → `P787-5`.

### LATAM

🟢 **The region where teachers out-adopt the OECD and institutions have no governance — the clearest
readiness-gap engagement in this base.**

- **Teacher adoption beats the OECD average by ~20 points** (TALIS 2024, AI use in the past year):
  **Brazil `56%`, Chile `55%`, Colombia `53%`, Costa Rica `52%`** against an **OECD average of `36%`**.
- 🔴 **Governance has not followed:** `87%` of regional universities use AI, **`26%`** have a formal
  strategy, **`9%`** evaluate implementation (UNESCO IESALC). **<`10%`** of institutions have both
  guidelines and the capacity to apply them (UNESCO).
- **Brazil, school level (Cetic.br):** `53%` of school administrators use AI; 🔴 only **`22%`** report
  their school has guidelines on its use.
- **Convening bodies, not regulators:** UNESCO launched the **Observatory on AI in Education for
  Latin America and the Caribbean** on `2026-04-14` in Santiago — partners include **CAF**, Chile's
  **CENIA**, Brazil's **CETIC.br**, ECLAC, **Tecnológico de Monterrey**, Fundación Santillana and
  ProFuturo. UNESCO also runs a **Mexico pilot with CONALEP and DGETI** and has an agreement with
  Tec de Monterrey.
- **Funded demand:** **Google.org's `$4.6M`** to the Raspberry Pi Foundation for *Experience AI*
  across Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, the Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Mexico, Peru and
  Uruguay — target **1.25M students by 2028**; local delivery partners **Sincroniza Educação** (BR),
  **Fundación Kodea** (CL), **Educación para Compartir** (MX).
- **OSS anchors here:** 🟢 `Frangersal/TutoriasITA` (MIT, TecNM Acapulco), `ftrujillo36/TutorLSM`
  (Apache-2.0, Mexican Sign Language), `goyanedelv/paes-mcp` (MIT, Chile's PAES admissions),
  `Matias-Sanmiguel/teams-facultad-mcp` (MIT); plus Chamilo and RosarioSIS as the incumbent
  Spanish-language platform layer.
- 🔴 **Named gap, and it is regulatory rather than technical:** no AI-in-education-specific national
  statute was found for **Brazil, Mexico or Chile**. The governing layer is UNESCO-convened soft
  governance, not law. 🔵 **Consequence for an engagement:** there is no compliance deadline to sell
  against here — the buying trigger is the **87% / 26% / 9%** gap and institutional risk, not a
  regulator.
- 🔵 **`T837` re-confirmed and sharpened by `T848`:** the apparent LATAM supply gap is partly an
  artefact of this base's own instruments. Chile's `paes-mcp` is real, MIT and sits at `0★` — below
  every star floor — **and its MIT grant is written in Spanish, which an English-only clause probe
  scored `UNGRADED`.** 🟢 A star floor and an English-only licence probe will both under-report
  LATAM, and this pass fixed the second.
- 🟢 **The offer:** an institutional AI-readiness and governance programme, priced as a diagnostic
  plus remediation, using the UNESCO Observatory's indicators as the external frame and Kolibri
  (MIT, offline-first) where connectivity is the constraint. See `compose/patterns.md` → `P787-3`
  and `P787-6`.

### Global

- 🟢 **The two leading education agents are permissive** — DeepTutor (Apache-2.0) and OpenMAIC (MIT)
  — so a studio can build on the state of the art without a licensing negotiation. That is unusual
  and worth saying to a client plainly.
- 🔴 **The platform layer is copyleft** (Moodle GPL-3.0, Open edX / Canvas / Frappe LMS AGPL-3.0).
  Every global engagement therefore turns on the same architectural decision: **attach across a
  process boundary (LTI via `csev/tsugi`, Apache-2.0) or embed only into a permissive host**
  (Kolibri, Sakai, OpenOLAT, nbgrader). See `verticals/solutions.md`.
- 🟢 **Assessment is the regulated surface in every region that regulates at all** — EU Annex III
  (grading, admissions, proctoring), Vietnam (automated assessment), Oklahoma (grading, discipline,
  promotion), New York (grade floor). 🟢 **A grading architecture with a human decision-maker and an
  audit trail is the one deliverable that ports across all four regions.**
- **Direction of travel:** the OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook recommends moving **beyond
  general-purpose AI tools toward purpose-built educational AI** with durable learning gains — which
  is the brief for a studio rather than for a chatbot licence.
