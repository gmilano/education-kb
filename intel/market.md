The 2026 baseline is a **narrow band ($8.7–10.6B)** and the long-range forecasts differ by
roughly **4×**. Nobody disagrees about the size of the market today; they disagree about
whether it is a $32B market in 2030 or a $137B market in 2035. **Quote the baseline, never the
horizon** — the horizon tells a client which vendor's blog you read. Technavio is the only one
of the five that explains its number structurally: a shift *"from experimental applications of
generative AI to the strategic deployment of autonomous agentic workflows"*, with grading and
scheduling as the named automation targets. That framing is worth more than the figure.

**Read these as direction, not as magnitude.** Every market-size figure located this pass
traces back to a vendor or aggregator blog; none traces to a primary research house
document we could open. Prior versions of this file published a five-row market-size table
with named research houses — those attributions did not survive re-checking and have been

| Country | Teachers using AI | | |

Four LATAM countries sit **16–20 points above the OECD average**. The region is not a
late adopter in this category; it is an early adopter with no governance layer.

**The commercial read, and it is the clearest single opportunity in this file:** **87% adopting
against 26% with a strategy** is a **61-point governance deficit**. The work that gap implies —
AI-use policy, model inventories, human-review design, assessment-integrity controls, staff
training, procurement criteria — is advisory-plus-implementation work that **does not require
displacing an incumbent platform**, which is the usual blocker in public-sector education. It
is also the work that becomes mandatory the moment Brazil's `PL 2338` passes with
education-related selection classified high-risk.

**Institutional entry points named in the sources:** UNESCO IESALC (with UNU-IAS), UNESCO
Santiago and Quito, **ECLAC**, FLACSO Ecuador, Uruguay's **Ceibal**, the Digital Education
Council (2026 LATAM survey, 29 institutions) and the **Latin American AI Index (ILIA)**.
UNESCO launched its **Observatory on AI in Education for Latin America and the Caribbean on
2026-04-14**, in Santiago at ECLAC's 2026 forum — a convening body that did not exist for
earlier passes and is a legitimate route into ministry-level conversations.

## Opportunities by region

### North America

**Pass 266 addition — the assessment-integrity opening is now permissively tooled.** Oklahoma's
`SB 1734` (educator supervision, human review, AI barred from high-stakes decisions, annual
parent disclosure), Ohio's `HB 96` (board-approved AI policy in every district) and California's
`AB 1159` (no student data for model training) all describe the *same* technical requirement:
**an assessment system whose AI is auditable, overridable and switchable off.**
[`ls1intum/Artemis`](https://github.com/ls1intum/Artemis) (MIT) is that shape by construction —
its Iris/Athena/Hyperion subsystems are **config-gated**, so "AI off" is a deployment setting
rather than a rebuild, and `AB 1159` compliance is a configuration claim a district can verify.
Pair with [`jupyter/nbgrader`](https://github.com/jupyter/nbgrader) (BSD-3-Clause) for the
notebook-based courses that dominate US data-science teaching. **The pitchable sentence:** a
district can adopt AI assistance and demonstrate, by configuration, that no high-stakes decision
was made by a model.

The largest market (36% share) and by far the most fragmented regulatory surface:
134 bills across 31 states means a national product does not exist — **compliance
configurability is the product**.

- **Per-jurisdiction policy enforcement as a feature.** `AB 1159`-style data-training bans,
  Oklahoma/Maryland human-oversight mandates and NYC's grade-banded moratorium are
  contradictory by design. Moodle 5.1's per-course/per-activity AI toggles map almost exactly
  onto this requirement; wire district policy to those toggles and the audit trail writes itself.
- **Human-in-the-loop assessment.** Where AI is barred from high-stakes decisions, the
  sellable architecture is `Artemis` (MIT, automated *feedback*) plus explicit teacher sign-off
  — not autonomous grading. `Claw-ED`'s teacher-reviewed posture is the right default.
- **Incumbent migration.** Canvas and Blackboard renewals are the natural trigger for an
  Open edX or Moodle migration with a client-owned AI layer.
- **AI literacy curriculum delivery.** `H.R. 8747` would let schools spend federal funds on AI
  curriculum. If it passes, procurement follows, and `instructional_agents` (MIT) is the
  content-generation engine for exactly that.
- **Policy exists, governance does not — and that is the retainer.** Roughly **34 states plus
  DC and Puerto Rico** have issued AI guidance, while **86% of education organisations use
  generative AI and most have no policy**. Reported student use is **92% of university
  students and 54% of K-12 students**. The gap between use and governance is not a product
  gap; it is an advisory-plus-implementation engagement, and it has a deadline attached in
  every state that now *mandates* a district policy (**Ohio `HB 96`**, **Idaho `SB 1227`**).
- **"AI may not replace a teacher" is an architectural requirement, not a slogan.** Idaho's
  enacted `SB 1227` says it outright, Oklahoma's `SB 1734` requires educator supervision plus
  **annual parent disclosure**. Build the disclosure artefact and the human-sign-off record as
  first-class features — `Claw-ED`'s teacher-reviewed posture plus an `lrsql`/xAPI audit trail
  — and one architecture satisfies Idaho, Oklahoma and NYC at once.
- **Higher education is the under-served half.** Arizona's `HB 4040` would push student-AI-use
  policies into public universities and New Jersey's bill would require colleges to *offer* AI
  certificate and degree programmes. Both are bills, not law; both point at university
  procurement with nobody incumbent. `Artemis` (MIT) and `Autolab` (Apache-2.0) are the
  permissive university-grade bases, and `nbgrader` (BSD-3) is already inside the data-science
  departments that will ask first.

### EMEA

**Pass 266 addition — EMEA is where the permissive supply actually comes from, which inverts the
usual framing.** Both assessment engines verified this pass are EMEA-origin and permissive:
**Artemis** (MIT, TU Munich, 819★, self-hosted by universities in several countries) and
**Numbas** (Apache-2.0, Newcastle University, 215★). Beside Richie/Marsha (MIT, France
Université Numérique) and OpenOLAT (Apache-2.0, Swiss), the EMEA engagement is **not** an import
of US tooling — the regionally-built, regionally-governed, permissively-licensed stack exists.
In a market where procurement asks about provenance and sovereignty, that is a differentiator
rather than a footnote.

**Numbas answers a data-residency objection structurally.** Its assessments execute **entirely
in the browser** — a Python 3 compiler emitting a JavaScript runtime — so a student submission
**need never reach a server**. Under GDPR that is a far stronger position than a processing
agreement over a cloud marking service, and it is the kind of architecture-level answer that
shortens a procurement cycle.

⚠️ **Governance is the gating constraint, not appetite:** one 2026 roundup reports **only ~10%
of 450+ surveyed institutions have formal AI-use guidelines** (secondary-source grade). The
**OECD 2026 Digital Education Outlook** recommends moving beyond general-purpose assistants
toward **purpose-built educational AI** — which is an argument, from a body ministries cite, for
exactly the Artemis/Iris-style deployment over a general chatbot rollout.

The clearest regulatory calendar in the world, and a **14-month window** before it closes.

- **The window is the offer.** High-risk obligations for education now bite on
  **2027-12-02**, not August 2026. Clients who read the delay as a reprieve will arrive late;
  the engagement is a compliance-by-design build *during* the window — classification of
  in-scope systems, human-oversight design, documentation.
- **Transparency is already live (2026-08-02).** Disclosure that a learner is talking to an
  AI, and deepfake disclosure, are obligations **today**. This is a short, concrete,
  immediately billable remediation scope.
- **Provenance as a procurement requirement.** `lineage-skill` (Apache-2.0) keeps generated
  material tied to its sources — the shape of evidence an AI Act file needs.
- **Sovereign deployment.** Moodle's AI subsystem pointed at local Ollama/vLLM means no
  student prompt leaves the member state. Pair with EMEA-origin, permissive bases:
  **OpenOLAT** (Apache-2.0, CH), **Artemis** (MIT, DE), **Richie/Marsha** (MIT, FR).
- **The market is ready**: nearly **two-thirds of European education systems** already have
  an AI strategy, guidance or policy framework, and two European Commission reports
  (2026-09-22) describe generative AI as a **mainstream** tool in EU education.
- **Two obligations the 2027 delay does *not* touch, and both are sellable now.** Emotion
  inference in schools is **in principle prohibited** — a prohibition, not a high-risk duty, so
  no deadline relief applies; any engagement touching attention-tracking, affect detection or
  "engagement scoring" from camera or voice must be audited against it **today**. And the
  **AI-literacy duty** on deploying organisations is already live. A prohibition audit plus a
  staff AI-literacy programme is a short, defensible first engagement that does not wait for
  December 2027.
- **Consent is a design constraint in the European Schools network.** Schola Europaea's
  guidelines mean **pupils cannot be required to use genAI** unless the tool is approved. Any
  learner-facing design needs a fully equivalent non-AI path — not a degraded fallback. Build
  it in from the first sprint; retrofitting an opt-out into an agentic flow is a rewrite.
- **Where EMEA clients self-host, they already reach for open weights.** 2026 deployment
  guidance describes districts with data-residency requirements self-hosting open-weight
  models while the mainstream uses hosted APIs. That is the exact architecture this KB
  recommends (Moodle AI subsystem or OpenOLAT against local Ollama/vLLM), which makes it an
  easier sale than it was a year ago.

### APAC

**Pass 266 addition — the APAC base is national, and it is MIT.** **Sunbird**
([`Sunbird-Ed/SunbirdEd-portal`](https://github.com/Sunbird-Ed/SunbirdEd-portal), MIT, 38,046
commits) is the stack beneath **DIKSHA**, India's national school platform under NCERT. For an
Indian public-sector engagement this reverses the default approach: the platform, the content
pipeline and the political precedent already exist, so the work is the **agent layer over a
national deployment** (see Pattern G in [`verticals/solutions.md`](../verticals/solutions.md)),
not a migration. It also lands directly on India's **AI-from-Class-3 mandate for AY 2026-27**,
where the delivery vehicle is already Sunbird-based.

**Vietnam is the most specific regulatory design brief in this file.** Its July 2026 decision
names **automated assessment** and **behavioural monitoring** as high-risk education uses, but
only *"where the AI output is the sole basis for a decision without meaningful human review."*
⚠️ (secondary-source grade). **Build the human review in and the system is not high-risk** —
Artemis's human-in-the-loop feedback model and config-gated AI are a direct fit. The deadline is
**2027-03-01**, with six extra months for systems already live before **2026-08-15**, so systems
being designed now are deciding their own classification.

**China constrains product shape, not paperwork:** anthropomorphic-AI rules bar virtual
intimate-relationship services for minors and require **parental consent under 14** ⚠️. A
companion-persona tutor — a common 2026 product instinct — is the wrong shape for that market;
a task-scoped tutor like Iris is not.

The largest mandated demand anywhere, and the thinnest open-source supply.

- **India is the single biggest curriculum event in the world**: AI and computational
  thinking compulsory **from Class 3 in every school from AY 2026-27**. That is a national
  content, teacher-training and assessment problem with a legislated deadline.
  `instructional_agents` (MIT) for materials at multilingual scale; `pykt-toolkit` for
  measuring whether any of it lands.
- **Teacher capacity is the bottleneck, not the models.** India is standing up curriculum
  through IIT Madras; Singapore is training every teacher including trainees by 2026. Teacher
  co-pilots (`Claw-ED`, `instructional_agents`) sell better here than student-facing tutors.
- **China's unevenness is the opportunity.** Beijing mandates 8 hours/year; the national
  rollout varies by province. Provincial-grade delivery and measurement is an unmet need,
  with the obvious caveat that China's AI rules are the region's most binding (registration,
  content controls, localisation).
- **Singapore as the regional reference deployment.** Highest adoption in APAC, a
  government platform (SLS) built on teacher supervision, and voluntary frameworks
  (AI Verify) that make piloting cheap. Win here, cite everywhere.
- **Korea is now the region's compliance market, and the clock is explicit.** The **AI Basic
  Act and its Enforcement Decree are in effect** with a grace period of **at least a year**
  before fines — the only APAC jurisdiction where an EU-style compliance-by-design engagement
  has a statutory hook. Korea is also 4th in regional adoption (40.6%). Pair the EMEA
  compliance playbook with Korean localisation and the offer is ready-made.
- **Vietnam has a national AI law in force** and 27.9% adoption — ahead of Japan. It is absent
  from most APAC education briefs, including this KB's earlier passes.
- **Correction to the supply story:** APAC is **not** supply-poor. The most-adopted education
  agent in open source, `HKUDS/DeepTutor` (41.5k, Apache-2.0), comes from the **Data
  Intelligence Lab at the University of Hong Kong**, and the learner-modelling layer below it
  is substantially Chinese-academic (`bigdata-ustc/EduData`, `HFUT-LEC/EduStudio`). What APAC
  has not produced is a **platform**. The engagement shape is therefore *integration of
  APAC-origin agents onto non-APAC platforms* — and in a Hong Kong, Singapore or mainland
  pitch, the headline component being local research is an asset, not a footnote.
- **Gap, stated plainly:** no APAC-origin open-source education **platform** surfaced. The
  earlier blanket claim ("no platform *or agent*") was wrong and is retracted in
  [`agents/top.md`](../agents/top.md).

### LATAM

**Pass 266 addition — the governance deficit is the offer.** The quantified section above is the
argument: **87% of surveyed LATAM higher-ed institutions use AI, 26% have a formal AI strategy**
⚠️, and Brazilian, Chilean, Colombian and Costa Rican teachers use AI **16–20 points above the
OECD average**. The engagement that gap implies — AI-use policy, model inventory, human-review
design, assessment-integrity controls, procurement criteria, staff training — **requires no
platform displacement**, which removes the usual public-sector blocker. It also pre-positions
the client for `PL 2338`, which would classify education-related selection as high-risk.

**Route in:** UNESCO's **Observatory on AI in Education for LAC** (launched 2026-04-14, Santiago,
at ECLAC), UNESCO IESALC, **Ceibal** (Uruguay) and the **ILIA** index are convening bodies, not
just citations — and the public/private split (**68%** public vs **84%** private non-profit ⚠️)
says the public sector is both the larger gap and the harder sale.

**On supply, stated precisely:** LATAM's open-source education output remains the **platform**
layer and it is **copylefted** — `portabilis/i-educar` (GPL-2.0, 34,948 commits) and `i-diario`
(AGPL-3.0). Pass 266 did verify a Portuguese-language *agent*
([`guedesindev/ai_tutor_forge`](https://github.com/guedesindev/ai_tutor_forge), GPL-3.0), but at
18★ and 21 commits it is a personal project — **do not brief it as regional capability.** For a
Brazilian municipal network the honest stack is i-Educar as the installed base plus a permissive
agent layer on top, with i-Diário's AGPL network clause priced before the SOW.

The region where the policy direction most favours what open source actually is.

- **Brazil's digital-sovereignty framing is a direct mandate for open source.** MEC/SEGAPE's
  March 2026 *Referencial* explicitly calls for national solutions that reduce dependence on
  global platforms. A self-hosted Moodle or Chamilo with local-model inference is not a
  cost compromise there — it is the stated policy preference.
- **Move before the resolution lands.** MEC is reported (2026-10-15) to be preparing a
  national resolution on AI in teaching based on CNE guidelines, not yet in the Diário
  Oficial. Institutions that build to the existing *Referencial* now will be compliant by
  construction; this is the moment to be in the room.
- **Teach-about-AI before teach-with-AI.** Brazil's MEC guidance orders it that way
  deliberately. Sell AI **literacy** curriculum and teacher formation first; student-facing
  tutors second. Pitching it in the other order misreads the buyer.
- **Mexico's gap is quantified and it is teacher training.** SEP's April 2026 survey:
  **76% of professors make materials with AI but only 36% teach students to use it**, and
  **9 in 10 want training**. There is **no public national AI competency standard for
  teachers**. MXN 284.1M is allocated to teacher professional development in 2026, and SEP
  announced three national encuentros on technology, phones and AI in schools (July 2026).
  A teacher-formation programme with a measurable competency framework is the highest-fit
  offer in the region.
- **Build on the installed base.** Chamilo has the deepest LATAM public-sector footprint;
  Moodle dominates the large public universities. Neither needs replacing — both need an
  agentic layer, delivered through an LTI tool so the platform licence stays untouched.
- **Adoption is not the obstacle.** Brazil and Mexico lead regional AI adoption (reported
  76% / 70%, August 2026) and 92% of LATAM university students already use AI. Governance
  and teacher capability are the obstacles, and both are services work.
- **The region publishes the platform layer, and this KB had missed it.**
  [`portabilis/i-educar`](https://github.com/portabilis/i-educar) (GPL-2.0, 719★, **34,948
  commits**, last commit **2026-10-14**) is a Brazilian school-management system that began in
  the *prefeitura de Itajaí (SC)* and was published on the federal **Portal do Software Público
  in 2008**; `i-diario` (AGPL-3.0, last commit **2026-10-09**) is its teacher portal. For a
  municipal or state network this is the installed base, with sovereignty provenance that no
  imported platform can match. Build the AI layer outside both, across an HTTP/LTI boundary,
  and the copyleft never reaches studio code.
- **Thirteen of nineteen LAC countries studied do not teach early AI adoption in schools**
  (CENIA, reported 2026). Read against Brazil's and Mexico's explicit teacher-formation
  programmes, this is the regional asymmetry: two markets are buying, most of the rest have not
  started. Pitch teacher formation in BR/MX and **AI-literacy curriculum** everywhere else —
  and note that the second is cheaper to deliver and politically easier to approve.
- **The funding is already placed, which tells you who to talk to.** **Google.org has committed
  $4.6M to AI-literacy rollout across nine LATAM countries including Brazil and Mexico,
  targeting 1.25M students by 2028.** UNESCO runs a regional **AI-in-education Observatory for
  LAC** with a Mexican pilot through **CONALEP and DGETI** — the federal technical-education
  systems, not universities. The IDB has reviewed **193 regional AI initiatives** and found
  **59% using generative AI and 27% using language models**. Multilateral and philanthropic
  money is funding literacy and teacher capability; implementation partners are what it lacks.
- **Brazil is converging on the EU, so EMEA work ports.** Brazil signed an **AI and
  tech-regulation agreement with the EU (June 2026)**, and `PL 2338` would classify
  **education-related selection** — admissions, placement — as **high-risk**, the same surface
  the EU's Annex III names. An AI-Act-shaped compliance artefact built for an EMEA client is
  substantially reusable in Brazil. Say this to clients operating in both; it is the cheapest
  credible differentiator available in the region.
- **Agentic infrastructure is the gap, not AI interest.** HolonIQ's 2026 LATAM EdTech 100 shows
  AI positioning concentrated in **teacher-facing** tools, with learning platforms losing more
  ground than any other category. Brazil's **Teachy** has taken that niche regionally and
  expanded into Asia. Competing on consumer tutoring in LATAM means competing with a funded
  incumbent in the one category the market has already picked; the open lane is the
  institutional plumbing — SIS-grade data, dropout prediction, compliance artefacts — that
  Teachy does not sell.
