The 2026 baseline is a **narrow band ($8.7–10.6B)** and the long-range forecasts differ by
roughly **4×**. Nobody disagrees about the size of the market today; they disagree about
whether it is a $32B market in 2030 or a $137B market in 2035. **Quote the baseline, never the
horizon** — the horizon tells a client which vendor's blog you read. Technavio is the only one
of the five that explains its number structurally: a shift *"from experimental applications of
generative AI to the strategic deployment of autonomous agentic workflows"*, with grading and
scheduling as the named automation targets. That framing is worth more than the figure.

**Read these as direction, not as magnitude.** Every market-size figure located this pass

| Country | Teachers using AI | | |
|---------|------------------|---|---|

Four LATAM countries sit **16–20 points above the OECD average**. The region is not a
late adopter in this category; it is an early adopter with no governance layer.

**The commercial read, and it is the clearest single opportunity in this file:** **87% adopting
against 26% with a strategy** is a **61-point governance deficit**. The work that gap implies —
AI-use policy, model inventories, human-review design, assessment-integrity controls, staff
training, procurement criteria — is advisory-plus-implementation work that **does not require
displacing an incumbent platform**, which is the usual blocker in public-sector education. It
is also the work that becomes mandatory the moment Brazil's `PL 2338` passes with
education-related selection classified high-risk.

**Institutional entry points named in the sources:** UNESCO IESALC (with UNU-IAS), UNESCO
Santiago and Quito, **ECLAC**, FLACSO Ecuador, Uruguay's **Ceibal**, the Digital Education
Council (2026 LATAM survey, 29 institutions) and the **Latin American AI Index (ILIA)**.
UNESCO launched its **Observatory on AI in Education for Latin America and the Caribbean on
2026-04-14**, in Santiago at ECLAC's 2026 forum — a convening body that did not exist for
earlier passes and is a legitimate route into ministry-level conversations.

## Opportunities by region

### North America

**Pass 266 addition — the assessment-integrity opening is now permissively tooled.** Oklahoma's
`SB 1734` (educator supervision, human review, AI barred from high-stakes decisions, annual
parent disclosure), Ohio's `HB 96` (board-approved AI policy in every district) and California's
`AB 1159` (no student data for model training) all describe the *same* technical requirement:
**an assessment system whose AI is auditable, overridable and switchable off.**
[`ls1intum/Artemis`](https://github.com/ls1intum/Artemis) (MIT) is that shape by construction —
its Iris/Athena/Hyperion subsystems are **config-gated**, so "AI off" is a deployment setting
rather than a rebuild, and `AB 1159` compliance is a configuration claim a district can verify.
Pair with [`jupyter/nbgrader`](https://github.com/jupyter/nbgrader) (BSD-3-Clause) for the
notebook-based courses that dominate US data-science teaching. **The pitchable sentence:** a
district can adopt AI assistance and demonstrate, by configuration, that no high-stakes decision
was made by a model.

The largest market (36% share) and by far the most fragmented regulatory surface:
134 bills across 31 states means a national product does not exist — **compliance
configurability is the product**.

- **Per-jurisdiction policy enforcement as a feature.** `AB 1159`-style data-training bans,
  Oklahoma/Maryland human-oversight mandates and NYC's grade-banded moratorium are
  contradictory by design. Moodle 5.1's per-course/per-activity AI toggles map almost exactly
  onto this requirement; wire district policy to those toggles and the audit trail writes itself.
- **Human-in-the-loop assessment.** Where AI is barred from high-stakes decisions, the
  sellable architecture is `Artemis` (MIT, automated *feedback*) plus explicit teacher sign-off
  — not autonomous grading. `Claw-ED`'s teacher-reviewed posture is the right default.
- **Incumbent migration.** Canvas and Blackboard renewals are the natural trigger for an
  Open edX or Moodle migration with a client-owned AI layer.
- **AI literacy curriculum delivery.** `H.R. 8747` would let schools spend federal funds on AI
  curriculum. If it passes, procurement follows, and `instructional_agents` (MIT) is the
  content-generation engine for exactly that.
- **Policy exists, governance does not — and that is the retainer.** Roughly **34 states plus
  DC and Puerto Rico** have issued AI guidance, while **86% of education organisations use
  generative AI and most have no policy**. Reported student use is **92% of university
  students and 54% of K-12 students**. The gap between use and governance is not a product
  gap; it is an advisory-plus-implementation engagement, and it has a deadline attached in
  every state that now *mandates* a district policy (**Ohio `HB 96`**, **Idaho `SB 1227`**).
- **"AI may not replace a teacher" is an architectural requirement, not a slogan.** Idaho's
  enacted `SB 1227` says it outright, Oklahoma's `SB 1734` requires educator supervision plus
  **annual parent disclosure**. Build the disclosure artefact and the human-sign-off record as
  first-class features — `Claw-ED`'s teacher-reviewed posture plus an `lrsql`/xAPI audit trail
  — and one architecture satisfies Idaho, Oklahoma and NYC at once.
- **Higher education is the under-served half.** Arizona's `HB 4040` would push student-AI-use
  policies into public universities and New Jersey's bill would require colleges to *offer* AI
  certificate and degree programmes. Both are bills, not law; both point at university
  procurement with nobody incumbent. `Artemis` (MIT) and `Autolab` (Apache-2.0) are the
  permissive university-grade bases, and `nbgrader` (BSD-3) is already inside the data-science
  departments that will ask first.

**Pass 267 — the North America section had the market structurally wrong, and the fix enlarges it.**

- 🔴 **Canvas is open source, and this KB said otherwise.** [`instructure/canvas-lms`](https://github.com/instructure/canvas-lms)
  is **AGPL-3.0 with 78,765 commits** — the largest education codebase this KB has located. The
  previous framing (Canvas as closed SaaS, *"the incumbent to migrate off"*) would have produced a
  needlessly adversarial pitch into the dominant platform of North American higher education. **The
  opening is the opposite of migration:** deploy or extend Canvas, reach it through
  [`vishalsachdev/canvas-mcp`](https://github.com/vishalsachdev/canvas-mcp) (**MIT**, 295★, 102–126
  tools), and keep the deliverable outside the AGPL boundary. **AGPL platform, MIT doorway** — no
  platform decision asked of the client, which is the fastest engagement shape in this file.
- 🟢 **The permissive assessment stack here is deeper than the regulatory requirement.** NA-origin
  and all permissive: [`Submitty`](https://github.com/Submitty/Submitty) (BSD-3, RPI, 9,316

  [`Autolab`](https://github.com/autolab/Autolab) (Apache-2.0, CMU), `nbgrader` (BSD-3), on

### EMEA

**Pass 266 addition — EMEA is where the permissive supply actually comes from, which inverts the
usual framing.** Both assessment engines verified this pass are EMEA-origin and permissive:
**Artemis** (MIT, TU Munich, 819★, self-hosted by universities in several countries) and
**Numbas** (Apache-2.0, Newcastle University, 215★). Beside Richie/Marsha (MIT, France
Université Numérique) and OpenOLAT (Apache-2.0, Swiss), the EMEA engagement is **not** an import
of US tooling — the regionally-built, regionally-governed, permissively-licensed stack exists.
In a market where procurement asks about provenance and sovereignty, that is a differentiator
rather than a footnote.

**Numbas answers a data-residency objection structurally.** Its assessments execute **entirely
in the browser** — a Python 3 compiler emitting a JavaScript runtime — so a student submission
**need never reach a server**. Under GDPR that is a far stronger position than a processing
agreement over a cloud marking service, and it is the kind of architecture-level answer that
shortens a procurement cycle.

⚠️ **Governance is the gating constraint, not appetite:** one 2026 roundup reports **only ~10%
of 450+ surveyed institutions have formal AI-use guidelines** (secondary-source grade). The
**OECD 2026 Digital Education Outlook** recommends moving beyond general-purpose assistants
toward **purpose-built educational AI** — which is an argument, from a body ministries cite, for
exactly the Artemis/Iris-style deployment over a general chatbot rollout.

The clearest regulatory calendar in the world, and a **14-month window** before it closes.

- **The window is the offer.** High-risk obligations for education now bite on
  **2027-12-02**, not August 2026. Clients who read the delay as a reprieve will arrive late;
  the engagement is a compliance-by-design build *during* the window — classification of
  in-scope systems, human-oversight design, documentation.
- **Transparency is already live (2026-08-02).** Disclosure that a learner is talking to an
  AI, and deepfake disclosure, are obligations **today**. This is a short, concrete,
  immediately billable remediation scope.
- **Provenance as a procurement requirement.** `lineage-skill` (Apache-2.0) keeps generated
  material tied to its sources — the shape of evidence an AI Act file needs.
- **Sovereign deployment.** Moodle's AI subsystem pointed at local Ollama/vLLM means no
  student prompt leaves the member state. Pair with EMEA-origin, permissive bases:
  **OpenOLAT** (Apache-2.0, CH), **Artemis** (MIT, DE), **Richie/Marsha** (MIT, FR).
- **The market is ready**: nearly **two-thirds of European education systems** already have
  an AI strategy, guidance or policy framework, and two European Commission reports
  (2026-09-22) describe generative AI as a **mainstream** tool in EU education.
- **Two obligations the 2027 delay does *not* touch, and both are sellable now.** Emotion
  inference in schools is **in principle prohibited** — a prohibition, not a high-risk duty, so
  no deadline relief applies; any engagement touching attention-tracking, affect detection or
  "engagement scoring" from camera or voice must be audited against it **today**. And the
  **AI-literacy duty** on deploying organisations is already live. A prohibition audit plus a
  staff AI-literacy programme is a short, defensible first engagement that does not wait for
  December 2027.
- **Consent is a design constraint in the European Schools network.** Schola Europaea's
  guidelines mean **pupils cannot be required to use genAI** unless the tool is approved. Any
  learner-facing design needs a fully equivalent non-AI path — not a degraded fallback. Build
  it in from the first sprint; retrofitting an opt-out into an agentic flow is a rewrite.
- **Where EMEA clients self-host, they already reach for open weights.** 2026 deployment
  guidance describes districts with data-residency requirements self-hosting open-weight
  models while the mainstream uses hosted APIs. That is the exact architecture this KB
  recommends (Moodle AI subsystem or OpenOLAT against local Ollama/vLLM), which makes it an
  easier sale than it was a year ago.
