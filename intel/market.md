## Structural read of the market

Three facts shape every engagement:

1. **Usage has decisively outrun governance.** 86% of education organisations use generative AI, and most lack a policy. One 2026 benchmark reports 54% of K-12 students and 92% of university students using AI. The buying trigger in 2026 is frequently *governance*, not capability — institutions already have AI in the building and no control over it.
2. **Agent deployment is still rare.** The Bridges 2026 survey of K-12 and college leaders found relatively few districts and colleges running AI agents in day-to-day operations, with leaders explicitly wary of giving agents access to sensitive student data. The gap between "using chatbots" and "running agents" is where the integration work is.
3. **The evidence base is thin, and buyers know it.** A July 2026 scoping review of 474 studies found the field fragmented, with pedagogical design poorly connected to technical capability. A 2025 study found AI tutors adapt worse in real time than human tutors. Expect pilots with measurement attached, not big-bang rollouts.

#### 🆕 p682 — the leg returned **one** fact this page did not hold, and it is a funding rule that creates a reporting obligation

🟢 **New, and it is the only genuinely new regional fact of this pass: `Workforce Pell`.** The US Department of Education's **final rule** creates the **Workforce Pell Grant Program**, extending Pell eligibility to **short-term programmes for the first time**. Dates, as the rule states them: provisions **generally effective `2026-07-20`**, with **optional early implementation from `2026-07-01`** at each institution's discretion. Scope: programmes of roughly **`150`–`599` clock hours** (about **8–15 weeks**), offered **only by accredited institutions**, with **governors in consultation with state workforce boards** designating the high-demand industries and approving programmes. 🔵 **Eligibility widens in a way traditional Pell does not:** it can reach students who **already hold a bachelor's degree**, and awards are **prorated by programme length**.

🟢 **Why this belongs in a market file and not only in a policy note — it is an engineering requirement with a date on it.** A Workforce Pell programme keeps its eligibility only by passing **completion, outcome and earnings tests per programme**, and a programme with zero or negative value-added earnings **loses eligibility**. That is **per-programme, longitudinal, auditable outcome reporting** — completion, credential attainment, and earnings linkage — demanded of exactly the institutions (community colleges, technical colleges, continuing-education arms) whose student-records estate is thinnest. 🔵 **This base already holds the layer that answers it:** the xAPI/LRS shelf ([`yetanalytics/lrsql`](https://github.com/yetanalytics/lrsql), `144★`, and [`adlnet/xAPI-Spec`](https://github.com/adlnet/xAPI-Spec), `955★`), the interoperability shelf ([`Ed-Fi-Alliance-OSS/edfi-oneroster`](https://github.com/Ed-Fi-Alliance-OSS/edfi-oneroster), [`jdolny/OneRoster.NET`](https://github.com/jdolny/OneRoster.NET)) and the early-warning row `p681` placed here ([`Saidkhusayn/student-dropout-early-warning`](https://github.com/Saidkhusayn/student-dropout-early-warning), MIT). **The buying trigger is a statutory deadline, not an AI ambition** — which is the most durable kind this file records.
