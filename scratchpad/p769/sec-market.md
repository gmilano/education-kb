## 🆕 `p769` — **School-estate decarbonisation and energy reporting. All four regions set a cadence — the second consecutive register to manage it — but this is the first register where the duty is a *published figure*, and that changes who buys and what can go wrong. 🔴 The EMEA duty currently exists in EU law and in NO member state's national law: the Commission opened infringement proceedings against all `27` on `15 July 2026`.**

**Every interval, threshold and effective date below is search-sourced and labelled as such. No primary legal text was read this pass (see `intel/open-gaps.md`).**

### 🔴 `T679` revisited, and sharpened rather than restated

`T679` held that *"North America refuses to set a cadence"*. `p768` found a hard federal cadence for fleet compliance (49 CFR 396.17) and concluded the refusal was a property of estate safety, not of the United States. 🟢 **This register sharpens it further: North America sets a cadence here too, but at STATE and CITY level, with no federal instrument at all.**

🟢 **The predictor that survives both data points: the US legislates federally where the duty rides on interstate commerce (a vehicle crosses state lines) and leaves it to states where the duty rides on a building (which does not).** A fleet duty is federal; a building duty is municipal. **For a studio this is operational, not academic: a North American fleet deliverable has one rule set, and a North American estate deliverable has as many rule sets as the client has jurisdictions** (`T713`).

## Opportunities by region

### North America

🟢 **`p769` — the strongest permissive substrate of any region on this register, and the only one with a published system of record.** `SEED-platform/seed` (BSD-3-Clause, `121★`, pushed `2026-11-11`) is a benchmarking platform built for exactly this duty, with `py-seed` (MIT **and** BSD-3) as its API client; `NatLabRockies/EnergyPlus` and `OpenStudio` (both BSD-3) are the engine and SDK; `LBNL-ETA/EnergyPlus-MCP` (BSD-3, `126★`) already exposes the engine to an agent. 🟢 **The opportunity is jurisdiction multiplication:** Washington's Phase 1 starts `1 June 2026` and covers K-12 explicitly, and a district spanning several jurisdictions needs one data model with per-jurisdiction cadence and threshold rules — which is `P352`. 🔴 **The named risk is the instrument itself:** ENERGY STAR is reported to be moving out of the EPA as of March 2026, with at least `$33M` appropriated for FY2026; **a deliverable that hard-codes Portfolio Manager as its scoring authority is exposed to a programme transfer**, so the score belongs behind an interface (`P353`).

### EMEA

🔴 **`p769` — the largest regulatory opportunity and the largest regulatory uncertainty on this register, and they are the same fact.** The **EPBD recast was due to be transposed by `29 May 2026`, and on `15 July 2026` the Commission opened infringement proceedings against all `27` member states** for failing to transpose it fully; a March 2026 action against `19` member states over draft National Building Renovation Plans preceded it. 🔴 **So an EMEA deliverable today is built against a directive, not against `27` national laws — the national detail a compliance engine needs does not exist yet in most member states.** 🟢 **The correct posture is therefore to build the EU-level obligations that are already dated and will not move (`31 Dec 2026` solar on new public buildings `>250 m²`, `1 Jan 2028` zero-emission new public buildings) and to keep national thresholds as configuration, not code.** 🟢 **The supply side is unusually good here:** `BBSR-GEG-Portal`'s MIT web service and print application are **the German statutory certificate artefact published permissively by the federal institute itself**, `ABC-TransitionBasCarbone/bilan-carbone` is MIT from the body that owns the French method, `energiacte/cteepbd` and `pachi/envolventecte` implement the Spanish CTE, and `jmcaamanog/CE3X-MCP-Server` already wraps the Spanish certification tool as an MCP server.

### APAC

🔵 **`p769` — the clearest duty on the register and the thinnest supply, and the two facts are unrelated.** Japan's 省エネ法 sets the most precisely dated cadence found anywhere this pass (届出 end of May, 定期報告 end of July, EEGS e-filing, **MEXT named as the competent ministry for schools**), and Australia has a schools rating tool plus a three-year public-school rating commitment. 🔴 **And the open-source supply in the region's own languages is nil:** `7` Japanese legs and `3` Korean legs returned **`0`, `0`, `0`, `0`, `0`, `0`, `0`** and **`1`, `1`, `0`**. 🟢 **The opportunity is consequently integration, not assembly:** a Japanese engagement is an **EEGS submission pipeline** over a permissive Western stack — `OpenStudio` for the model, `rrule` for the two statutory dates, and the 1,500 kL threshold as a volume trigger rather than a floor-area one. 🔵 Australia's NABERS commitment is a **rating-production** engagement with a fixed three-year horizon. 🔴 **No verdict is published for Korea or China on the school estate: the targeted search leg returned nothing on Korean or Chinese school energy instruments, and the Chinese-language supply legs are pollution-dominated (see below).**

### LATAM

🟢 **`p769` — the only region whose duty is a dated ratchet, which makes it the easiest to sell and the hardest to fake.** Brazil's **Resolução CGIEE nº 4/2025** puts federal public buildings at ENCE level `A` from `2027` and NZEB by `2035`, states from `2032`, municipalities above `50,000` to `2040`, requires the labels in **public procurement**, and requires the **ENCE to be displayed at the main entrance** of a completed public building. 🟢 **A ratchet with named years is a multi-year engagement with a built-in renewal: the client cannot do it once.** 🔴 **Supply is the weakest of the four regions:** the Portuguese and Spanish legs returned `17`, `8`, `1`, `2` and a `450`-result `huella de carbono` leg that is almost entirely coursework, with **`0` grant-carrying Brazilian or Spanish-American repositories on this register**. 🔵 **`OneBus/OneBus-API` (MIT, LATAM) carried forward from `p768` remains the only LATAM-origin permissive data model on this base's recent registers**, and it is about vehicles, not buildings. 🔴 **Schools are not named in Resolução 4/2025** — their inclusion is an inference from "public building", and a Brazilian engagement should confirm it against the resolution text before it is scoped.

### Global

🔵 **`p769` — the market numbers do not agree on their own base year, and that is the finding to carry into a proposal.** Building energy management software forecasts cluster at **`9–14%` CAGR**: one estimate puts BEMS at **`$46.1B` in 2026 → `$83.77B` by 2032 at `10.43%`**, another puts building-specific software at **`$2.49B` (2025) → `$6.51B` (2032) at `13.9%`**, and broader energy management software estimates for 2026 range from **`$14.29B`** to **`$60.94B`** depending on scope. 🔴 **Carbon accounting software is worse: 2026 base-year estimates in the same search range from `$98.1M` to `$22.27B` to `$27.51B` — a spread of more than `100×` — with CAGRs from `4.01%` to `22.2%`.** 🟢 **A `100×` disagreement is not noise, it is the absence of an agreed market definition**, and a studio quoting any single figure is quoting a scope choice. 🔵 **The defensible framing for a client conversation is the duty, not the market: the dates are fixed (`31 Dec 2026`, `1 Jun 2026`, `1 Jan 2028`, `2027`, `2035`), and the spend follows them.** 🔴 **Regulatory headwind, global:** the CSRD **Omnibus** raised thresholds to **`>1,000` employees and `>€450M` turnover from FY2027**, which one vendor-adjacent source puts at roughly **`85%` of previously covered companies removed from mandatory reporting** — a single-source figure this base does not endorse. **The school estate is largely unaffected, because its duty comes from building law, not from corporate reporting law — which makes the public estate a more durable buyer than the corporate one** (`T714`).
