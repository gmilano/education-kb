🔴 **Disposition: refused, on the licence and not on the quality.** GPL-2-or-3 reaches a derivative work, so a client-branded authoring platform built on this tree carries a source-disclosure obligation. That is the same verdict this shelf gives Moodle and Chamilo, and it is a licence fact rather than a judgement about the software, which is excellent.

🆕 **The new defect class — `grant by reference, with the referenced text absent.`** `COPYING` is a **notice**, not a licence: it states which licence governs and then says *"You should have received a copy of the GNU General Public License along with PreTeXt. If not, see <http://www.gnu.org/licenses/>."* 🔴 **You did not receive one. The repository contains no copy of the GPL at any of the five probed filenames.** GPL-2 §1 and GPL-3 §4 both condition redistribution on giving recipients a copy of the licence, so a downstream redistributor of this tree cannot satisfy the condition from the tree alone — they have to go and fetch the text themselves and guess which of the two versions to ship. 🔵 **Why this is distinct from the seven defect classes already on these shelves.** Those are defects in *what the grant says* — absent, self-contradictory, an unfilled template, truncated, a different project's terms. This one is a defect in *what the grant ships*: the terms are named unambiguously and correctly, by a licensor who clearly knows what he is doing, and the bytes that constitute them are not there. 🟡 **It also explains the `NOASSERTION`**: GitHub's detector matches licence *bodies*, and there is no body to match — so a repository whose licensing intent is perfectly clear in prose reads as unlicensed to every automated consumer.

🔵 **What to do with it on an engagement anyway, since the software is good and live.** Treat PreTeXt as a **tool you run**, not a tree you fork: it converts single-source XML into HTML, PDF and EPUB, and running a GPL program over a client's content imposes nothing on the content or on anything you build beside it. The copyleft only bites if you ship a derivative of PreTeXt itself. **Author with it, wrap it over a process boundary, and keep your own code on the other side** — the same posture `P51` takes toward copyleft LMS platforms.

### Pattern I — Launch over LTI, record over xAPI, never fork the platform (best fit: any client with an incumbent LMS) 🟢 new at p656

The pattern that makes the other eight safe to sell. Every pattern above eventually asks the same two questions — *how does our thing appear inside the LMS the client already runs,* and *how do we prove it worked* — and until p656 this shelf answered neither.

1. **Get in over LTI 1.3, not through a fork.** `tsugiproject/tsugi` (Apache-2.0) for PHP, `dmitry-viskov/pylti1.3` (MIT) for Python, `jupyterhub/ltiauthenticator` (BSD-3-Clause) when the destination is a notebook. One integration launches inside Moodle, Canvas, Brightspace, Open edX, Sakai and OpenOlat. 🔵 **The licence consequence is the whole point: an LTI tool is a separate work that the LMS merely launches, so a GPL-3.0 Moodle or an AGPL-3.0 Open edX imposes nothing on it.** This is the same boundary P3 draws around XBlocks, generalised to every platform at once.
2. **Keep the client's content where it is.** `jcputney/scorm-again` (MIT) plays their existing SCORM 1.2 / 2004 / AICC / cmi5 packages. If they author in H5P or Adapt, both are **GPL-3.0 — integrate, never fork**; call their APIs and ship nothing derived from them.
3. **Record every interaction to a permissive LRS.** `yetanalytics/lrsql` (Apache-2.0) behind `RusticiSoftware/TinCanJS` (Apache-2.0) on the content side, against the statement shapes in `adlnet/xAPI-Spec` (Apache-2.0). Actor, verb, object, result, timestamp — per learner, per attempt. 🟢 **This is the deliverable that turns a pilot into a renewal**, and it is what `compose/patterns.md` P5 has been selling as "governance and measurement" without naming a component.
4. **Assess with the permissive option where the subject allows it.** `numbas/Numbas` (Apache-2.0) for mathematical and quantitative work; `oat-sa/tao-core` (GPL-2.0) only where QTI compliance is contractually required, and then client-operated.
5. **Accessibility is a client-operated deployment, not a product.** `ucfopen/UDOIT` (GPL-3.0) is the only real tool. Deploy it in the client's estate, bill the remediation work, ship no derivative.

**Licence position end to end:** steps 1–4 are Apache-2.0 / MIT / BSD-3 and can carry proprietary work. Steps 2 (authoring) and 5 (accessibility) are copyleft and stay stock. **Timeline 4–6 weeks** on top of an existing engagement; it is plumbing, and the components are small.

🔴 **Scope the single point of failure explicitly.** `lrsql` is the only live permissive LRS in existence — `learninglocker` is GPL-3.0 and dormant since 2024-05-30, `adlnet/ADL_LRS` is ~12 months quiet. Name that dependency in the proposal.

## 🔴 p659 — the public 2026 LMS guides print the wrong licence for Sakai, and this page is right

The mandated vertical-platform leg returned the current crop of “best open-source LMS 2026” guides. Two of them state that **Sakai is Apache-2.0**, and one presents it as *the* permissively-licensed institutional option on that basis.

🟢 **This page already carries the correct cell, measured at first hand:** `sakaiproject/sakai` is **ECL-2.0** — the Educational Community License v2.0 — read from the payload at `11,120 B`. No cell was changed at p659; the external claim was checked against this base and the base held.

🔵 **The distinction is not pedantic, and it cuts in the direction clients care about.** ECL-2.0 is an Apache-2.0 *derivative*: the permissions are substantially Apache's, but the **patent grant is narrowed** — where Apache-2.0 grants patent rights for the work generally, ECL-2.0 scopes the contributor's patent grant to the contributions as used in the licensed work, with the educational-context framing the licence was written for. For a Globant engagement that means: Apache-2.0 and ECL-2.0 are **both** safe for a closed derivative, so the build decision does not change — but **a client's counsel diffing your licence schedule against the project's actual payload will find a discrepancy if you copied `Apache-2.0` out of a guide.** Write `ECL-2.0` and cite the payload.

🔴 **The general rule this instance evidences.** A secondary source's licence claim is worth exactly nothing against a payload read. Of the licence claims this pass's vertical leg served, the one that was checkable against this base's own first-hand reads was **wrong**, and it was wrong in the *permissive* direction — the direction that gets a deliverable shipped before anyone notices. That is the same failure mode as **T38**, arriving through a different channel: not GitHub's classifier this time, but published human guidance.
